1. Who This Applies To

This Privacy Policy describes how Poolnautics LLC ("Poolnautics," "we," "our," or "us") handles personal information in two distinct roles:

  • For Subscribers — the pool service businesses who hold accounts with us — Poolnautics acts as a data controller. We decide how and why your account information is collected and used.
  • For End Customers — the homeowners and property managers whose information is entered into Poolnautics by a Subscriber — Poolnautics acts as a data processor. The Subscriber is the data controller and decides how that information is used. We process End Customer data only on the Subscriber's behalf and according to their instructions.
  • For Subscriber personnel — the technicians, employees, independent contractors, and subcontractors who use the Poolnautics technician app for a Subscriber — Poolnautics acts as a data processor. The Subscriber decides whether the app's location features (described in Section 2) are used for each of its personnel, controls its personnel's data, and is responsible for giving them any notice, and getting any consent, the law requires. We process this data only on the Subscriber's behalf.

If you are a technician or other Subscriber personnel with a question or request about your location or other data, please contact the pool service company you work for first. You can also contact us at [email protected]; because the company controls this data, we will work with it to respond.

If you are an End Customer with a privacy question or request, please contact the pool service company that holds your account. We forward any direct requests we receive to the relevant Subscriber. Text messages a Subscriber sends through Poolnautics go out through the Subscriber's own text-messaging provider. To stop receiving them, reply STOP to any such message: the Subscriber's provider stops the messages, and when it reports the opt-out, Poolnautics adds your number to that Subscriber's do-not-text list. For help with a Subscriber's messages, contact that Subscriber.

Our Role Under U.S. State Privacy Laws (CCPA / CPRA and Similar)

For purposes of the California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended ("CCPA"), and analogous comprehensive privacy laws in other U.S. states, each Subscriber is the "business" with respect to the personal information of its End Customers, and Poolnautics is a "service provider" (or, where the applicable state uses different terminology, a "processor" or "contractor"). We process End Customer personal information only on the Subscriber's documented instructions and only for the limited and specified purposes set out in our agreement with the Subscriber and this Privacy Policy.

As a service provider, we will not: (a) sell or share End Customer personal information; (b) retain, use, or disclose it for any purpose other than the business purposes specified in our agreement or as otherwise permitted by the CCPA; (c) retain, use, or disclose it outside of the direct business relationship between Poolnautics and the Subscriber; or (d) combine it with personal information we receive from or on behalf of any other source, except as permitted by Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ag)(1) (for example, to detect security incidents or fraud). We assist Subscribers in responding to verified consumer rights requests through (i) a self-serve export, for the Subscriber's admins, of its customers, service history, invoices, payments, routes, and requests (see Section 12); (ii) self-serve in-app deletion of End Customer records that have no service, quote, or billing history; (iii) self-serve deletion of the Subscriber's whole account and its data (see Section 10); and (iv) a privacy support channel at [email protected] for requests the in-app tools do not cover, including deleting records that have history. We will respond to a Subscriber's privacy support request within the timeframe reasonably needed to meet the Subscriber's own statutory response deadline.

2. What Data We Collect

From Subscribers

  • Account information: name, email address, phone number, organization name, business address.
  • Signing in with Google or Apple: office users can create an account or sign in with Google or Apple, through our authentication provider, Supabase. We then receive their name, email address, and an ID for that Google or Apple account. We never receive their Google or Apple password, and we don't get access to anything else in that account. If they use Apple's Hide My Email, we receive a relay address that Apple forwards to them, in place of their real email address.
  • Billing information: billing contact and payment method details. Card data is handled by Stripe — we never see, receive, or store full card numbers, CVV codes, or other sensitive payment details.
  • Usage data: features used, pages visited, IP address, browser and device characteristics, sign-in timestamps, and other operational metadata.
  • QuickBooks Online connection: if a Subscriber connects QuickBooks Online, the QuickBooks company's ID and name; the access tokens Intuit issues, stored encrypted and never shown; the Subscriber's QuickBooks choices (start date, accounts, product or service, tax rate); and the QuickBooks IDs of the records Poolnautics has sent. We don't copy a Subscriber's books into Poolnautics.

From End Customers (entered by Subscribers, stored by Poolnautics)

  • Name, email address, phone number, service address, billing address.
  • Saved payment method metadata only — card brand, last four digits, expiry date. The full card lives on Stripe; we receive only the non-sensitive metadata Stripe returns after a save-card flow.
  • Service history, water-chemistry readings, technician notes, and photos uploaded during service visits.
  • Invoice, estimate, and payment history.

From Subscriber Personnel Using the Technician App

Technicians use the Poolnautics mobile app on their phones. The Subscriber they work for decides which of the app's features are used for each technician, and we process the data on the Subscriber's behalf (see Section 1).

Location, used only for the three purposes below, and only with the technician's permission on their phone:

  • Service visits. When a technician sends a service report or a "can't service" report, the app records the phone's location at that moment (using the phone's standard accuracy setting, not high-accuracy GPS) to confirm the visit. This applies to every Subscriber. The location is saved with the report, and the service report the Subscriber emails its customer includes a link showing it on a map.
  • Mileage logging, only when the Subscriber turns it on for that technician. While the technician's route is under way, from the first service report of the day until the route is finished (or 9 PM at the latest), the app records the phone's precise location in the background to work out the miles driven between stops. The raw location points stay on the phone and are deleted from it once the day's miles are worked out. What the Subscriber receives is each trip between stops: where it started and ended (a customer stop or one of the Subscriber's business locations), when it left and arrived, and its miles. It also receives the time and customer of each service report, how long the vehicle stopped anywhere else for 10 minutes or more, and how many location points the phone reported as simulated. The Subscriber can have weekly mileage summaries emailed to addresses it chooses.
  • Drive history ("Show full drive"), only when a Subscriber admin turns it on for a specific technician, for up to 7 days at a time. It ends automatically, and an admin can turn it on again. During company work hours (currently 7 AM to 7 PM in the Subscriber's time zone), on any day in that period, the app records the phone's precise location, including while the app is closed if the technician has set location access to "Always." The Subscriber can see the route driven, where the vehicle stopped for 10 minutes or more, any times the phone stopped reporting its location, and how many location points the phone reported as simulated. Location points recorded outside work hours are not kept, although each day's drive includes the locations of the service reports sent that day. While drive history is on, the phone may check its approximate location at low power outside work hours, only to know when the work day starts; those checks are not recorded or sent.

We do not collect location from the technician app for any other purpose, and we never sell location data or use it for advertising.

Technicians' choices. The app asks for the phone's permission before using location. Technicians can change or turn off the app's location access at any time in their phone's Settings; some features, such as visit confirmation and mileage logging, do not work without it. On Android phones, a notification shows while the app is recording location in the background. On iPhones, a blue location indicator shows when the app uses location in the background with location access set to "While Using"; with "Always," the app does not show it.

Simulated locations. When the phone reports that a location point recorded for mileage logging or drive history is simulated (for example, by an app that fakes the phone's location), the app does not use that point and records only how many such points there were. Android phones report this; iPhones do not. The count is shown to the Subscriber's admins with the technician's drive history.

On iPhones, if the Subscriber requires a technician's location access to be set to "Always," the app first shows the technician an agreement screen describing how their location is used, and records their agreement: the date, the wording shown, the app version, the phone's platform, and a random ID for the app's installation on that phone. Technicians can still change the setting on their phone at any time. On Android phones, the app doesn't use or ask for "Always" location: it records location only while a notification is showing.

No location:

  • App status ("phone health"). A random ID for the app's installation on that phone (not a hardware or advertising ID); the platform; the app's version, build, and update; the phone's model and operating system version; whether location services and location, background location, precise location, and notification permissions are on; whether app notifications are on and the phone is registered for them; whether battery saver is on; free and total storage; how many service reports and photos are waiting to send, and since when; and how far the phone's clock is off. Kept until 60 days after the phone last reports.
  • A push notification token, so the Subscriber can send alerts to the technician's phone, such as new or updated work orders, route changes, truck check-in and oil-change reminders, and, if the Subscriber uses fleet features, reminders before the license plate of the company vehicle the technician drives expires.
  • Photos and readings the technician adds, such as photos on service reports and work orders, and odometer readings and photos on truck check-ins.

Vehicle and fleet documents. If the Subscriber uses fleet features, the technician or the Subscriber may add information about the vehicle the technician drives for work: its make, model, and license plate; the plate's expiry date; its current mileage, next oil change, and oil change history; notes; photos of the vehicle, its odometer, and its oil-change sticker; a photo of its registration; and its insurance card (a photo, or a PDF added by the Subscriber's office) with the insurance expiry date. We also record who added the insurance card and when. These documents can include names, addresses, vehicle identification numbers, and insurance policy numbers. The Subscriber can choose to require its technicians to add the vehicle's make, model, plate, and plate expiry before they start their route in the app; this is off unless the Subscriber turns it on.

Who can see it. This data is available to the technician's company in the Poolnautics web app: drive history to its admins, and mileage records, app status, and the dates of location agreements to its admins and managers. Each technician can see their own mileage in the app. Vehicle records and documents are available to the company's owners, admins, and managers, who can view, add, and replace them, and each technician can see and update their own in the app. The company's web app users are notified in the web app before a plate or insurance expires and when a technician adds a new insurance card; these notices give the technician's name and the dates, not the documents. We don't sell vehicle records or documents or share them with advertisers. A service visit's location also appears in the service report the company emails its customer, as described above. Poolnautics accesses this data only as needed to operate and support the service. It is stored with our hosting and database providers, and to show a drive on a map, the locations where the vehicle stopped are sent to our mapping provider (see Section 6).

Information stored on the technician's phone. So the technician can work without a signal, the app keeps a copy of their route on the phone, from the day before through the next six days. That copy includes each customer's name, address, phone number, email address, gate code or access notes, and service notes, along with their work orders. Service reports, photos, and other items saved without a signal stay on the phone until they are sent. The app also keeps the technician's own mileage for the last 14 days, and the current day's location points until that day's miles are worked out.

If the Subscriber uses fleet features, the app also keeps the technician's vehicle record (make, model, plate, expiry dates, and mileage) and one copy each of the vehicle's insurance card and registration photo on the phone, so the technician can show them without a signal. The copies are updated when the technician opens the app's Fleet Management screen with a connection. If the Subscriber removes a document, its copy stays on the phone until the technician signs out or a new one is added. Like the app's other data, the copies may be included in the phone's backups, and anyone with access to the unlocked phone may be able to see them.

If the technician adds the app's home-screen widgets, they show: in the Next Stop widgets, the next stop (the customer's name and address) and the stops after it (customer names and street addresses); in the Work Orders widget, open work orders (the type of work, the customer's name, the customer's full service address, and how many days each has been open) and a count of the work orders waiting on the office, which aren't listed; and in the Shopping widgets, the technician's shopping list (item names and quantities). On iPhones, while a route is under way, the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island can show the next stop's number, the customer's name and address, the work orders at that stop, and route progress. None of these show prices or gate codes. Because customers' information appears on the phone, including on its Lock Screen, technicians should protect their phone with a passcode and keep that information private.

When a technician signs out of the app, it erases the saved route, the work orders, the widgets' information, and the vehicle record and the insurance card and registration copies from the phone, and the Lock Screen display ends. Anything not yet sent stays on the phone so the same technician can send it the next time they sign in, and the technician's recent mileage stays too; both are erased if the technician deletes their account.

Deleting a technician account. Technicians can delete their account from the app's Settings. Their login is deleted. Their name is replaced with "Former technician" (or "Former team member" for other staff), and their email address, phone number, username, PIN, home address, personal notes, and shopping list are removed. Their push notification tokens are deleted, and anything on that phone not yet sent, along with their mileage stored there, is erased. The Subscriber's admins and managers are notified by email. The Subscriber keeps its business records of the technician's work under "Former technician": service reports, with their locations, and their photos until those are deleted (see Section 10); work orders; mileage records; vehicle records and documents; location agreements; drive history, until it is deleted 30 days after it was recorded; and app status, until it is deleted 60 days after the phone last reported. Deleting the account of the Subscriber itself is described in Sections 10 and 12. To ask for an account to be deleted without the app, see Delete your account.

Deactivating a team member. An admin of the Subscriber can deactivate a technician or other team member from that person's page in Team. Their login is blocked, so they can no longer sign in to the Poolnautics web app or the technician app. Their open stops and open requests are unassigned, push notifications to their phone stop, and any unused invite or password-reset link is cancelled. Their name, email address, and phone number stay on the Subscriber's account, and their name stays on the work they already did, such as service reports and finished requests. These are kept while the person is deactivated, so the Subscriber can bring them back with Reactivate. They are deleted if the Subscriber removes the person from its team (Remove member), or when the Subscriber's account is deleted.

When the technician app stops working. If the Subscriber's plan ends, or its account is being deleted, or it deactivates a technician or removes them from its team, the technician app stops working for that technician the next time their phone contacts our server: they can't sign in, load their route, or start a stop. If the plan has ended, the app first tries to send a report for a visit already under way; otherwise, anything on the phone not yet sent is discarded. The app then signs the technician out and erases what it keeps on the phone for offline use: customers and their addresses, gate codes, photos, the saved route and work orders, unsent reports and report drafts, truck documents, mileage, and widgets. A phone that never contacts our server again can't be erased this way.

Error Reports and App Feedback (Poolnautics Technician App)

Error reports. The Poolnautics app sends a report to Poolnautics so we can find and fix the problem when:

  • the app crashes;
  • a screen in the app shows an error;
  • an upload fails and the app stops retrying it (for example, a service report that can't be sent);
  • your route fails to download several times in a row while our server is answering; or
  • signing in fails because of a problem on our side.

Everyday connection trouble, such as having no signal, never sends a report on its own. A report includes:

  • what went wrong (the error message and technical details, such as where in the app it happened, when, and, for an upload, what kind of upload it was and how many times it was tried);
  • the app version and update, your phone's model and operating system version;
  • the names of up to 8 app screens you opened recently (not what was on them);
  • your account and company IDs, so we can tell which account had the problem.

What reports never contain. The app never attaches customer records, chemical readings, photos or the contents of service reports to a report or to feedback. Separately, email addresses, phone numbers, labeled gate and access codes (such as "gate code 1234"), links, photo file names and sign-in tokens are removed from error messages and from what you type before anything leaves our servers. Error messages are also cleaned on your phone before they're sent.

Feedback. In Settings › Help & Feedback, you can report a problem with the app or suggest a feature. What you write goes to the Poolnautics team, along with your name and company so we can follow up with you, and the app version and update and your phone's model and operating system version. It doesn't go to your company's office. A problem report also includes the names of the app screens you opened recently, whether anything on your phone is waiting to upload, when your route last downloaded, and whether your phone was offline. Our error-monitoring provider receives only a reference number for a problem report, the app and phone details, the recent screen names, and how many uploads are waiting or couldn't be sent, with your account and company IDs; what you write goes only to our team's Slack. Please don't include customer details in what you write: names you type can't be removed automatically.

Without a signal, error reports wait on your phone for up to 7 days, and feedback for up to 14 days, until they can be sent.

How we use it. We use error reports and feedback only to find and fix problems and to improve the app. We don't use them for advertising, we don't use them to track you across other apps or websites, and we don't sell them.

Where it goes. Error reports and feedback pass through our own server first. Error reports and problem reports then go to Better Stack, our error-monitoring provider. Feedback, and alerts about the most serious errors (the app crashing, a service report that won't send, or signing in failing on our side), are posted to our team's Slack; those alerts include the error, the screen it happened on, the company ID and the app version. Both providers act on our behalf and only as needed to provide these services (see Section 6).

How long we keep it. We keep error reports and feedback only as long as we need them to investigate and fix problems, and then delete them.

From Website Visitors

  • IP address, browser type and version, pages visited, referring URL, and other standard server-log information used for security and basic analytics.
  • What you enter in our website's forms when you contact us, request a demo, apply to be a partner, or reserve a spot: your name, business name, email address, phone number, and your answers, such as how many customers you have and your message, along with your country and the page you sent it from. We use it to reply to you, and when you reserve a spot, we add you to our waiting list so we can send you updates.

4. How We Use Data

We use the information described above to:

  • Operate the service — sending invoices, processing payments, dispatching routes, generating service reports.
  • Provide the technician app features a Subscriber turns on — confirming visits, keeping mileage records, showing drive history, keeping vehicle records and documents in one place and showing them in the app, reminding technicians and the Subscriber before a license plate or insurance expires, and showing the Subscriber whether each phone's app is up to date and able to send — and sending alerts to technicians' phones.
  • Communicate with Subscribers about account activity, billing, product updates, and support requests.
  • Communicate with End Customers on a Subscriber's behalf — for example, sending invoice notifications, payment receipts, autopay decline notices, or service pause notices that the Subscriber has configured or chosen to send.
  • Maintain and improve the service, including diagnosing errors, measuring performance, and developing new features.
  • Detect, prevent, and respond to fraud, security incidents, and abuse.
  • Comply with legal obligations, including tax and accounting rules.

We do not sell personal information. We do not use End Customer data to advertise to End Customers.

Data Poolnautics reads from or sends to a Subscriber's QuickBooks Online is used only to keep that Subscriber's QuickBooks matching what is recorded in Poolnautics. We do not sell it, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models.

7. Data Security

We take reasonable technical and organizational measures to protect personal information, including:

  • HTTPS / TLS 1.2+ encryption for all traffic between users and Poolnautics.
  • Stripe handles all sensitive cardholder data in a PCI-DSS Level 1 compliant environment. Poolnautics never sees or stores full card numbers.
  • Database encryption at rest, provided by our database host.
  • QuickBooks access tokens are encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and bound to the Subscriber's account.
  • Row-level access controls so that staff at one Subscriber's organization cannot access another Subscriber's customer data.
  • Internal access restrictions — only authorized Poolnautics personnel can access systems containing personal information, and only for legitimate operational reasons.

No security measure is absolute. We cannot guarantee that personal information will never be compromised, but we work continuously to reduce risk.

8. Security Incident Notification

If we become aware of an unauthorized access, use, or disclosure of personal information that materially affects you, we will notify affected Subscribers without undue delay, and in any event within the timeframes required by applicable law.

Our notification will describe, to the extent then known: the nature of the incident, the categories of data involved, the likely consequences, and the steps we are taking in response. Where Subscriber action is required to mitigate risk to End Customers, we will provide the information Subscribers need to fulfill their own notification obligations as data controllers.

9. Cookies & Tracking

Poolnautics itself doesn't set cookies. We use your browser's storage and a few technologies from our providers:

When a Subscriber Leaves

A Subscriber's account becomes read-only when its plan ends: at the end of the period it paid for, if it cancels; 14 days after a subscription payment fails, if it's still unpaid (or sooner, if the subscription is ended or paused for non-payment); 7 days after its free trial ends, if it hasn't chosen a plan; or when Poolnautics ends its subscription under the Terms of Service.

  • Read-only for 60 days. The Subscriber's office users can view everything, and its admins can export it, but no one can change anything. No new invoices are created and automations stop, but invoices already sent keep working, including payment pages, autopay, and reminders. Its technicians can't use the technician app, which erases the Subscriber's information from their phones (see Section 2). Choosing a plan during the 60 days restores everything.
  • Reminders. 30 days and 7 days before the deletion date, we email the date to every admin and to the Subscriber's company email address.
  • Deleted after 60 days. 60 days after the account became read-only, or later if deletion waits (see below), we permanently delete the Subscriber's account and its data: its End Customers' data, service history, invoices and payment records, routes, requests, files (including photos, logos, mileage records, vehicle documents, and exports), and its users' logins. Its End Customers' customer records in its Stripe account are deleted too, including their saved cards and bank accounts. We then email its admins and its company email address that it's done.
  • Deleting sooner. Any admin of the Subscriber can delete its account and data at any time in the Poolnautics web app (Settings › Plan). To confirm, the admin types the company's name and enters a code we email to the Subscriber's company email address, not to the admin. Once confirmed, deletion is final and can't be undone: the Subscriber's plan ends at once, no one can sign in to the web app or the technician app, the technician app erases the Subscriber's information from technicians' phones and discards anything on them not yet sent (see Section 2), its admins are emailed, and the data is deleted, normally within a day. A Subscriber can also ask by emailing [email protected]; we confirm the request with the same kind of code, sent to the company email address, and delete the data within 30 days.
  • When deletion waits. We delay deletion while a card dispute is open, a payment is still processing, or the Subscriber owes money on its Stripe account or we can't confirm that it doesn't, and while we need to keep the data for a legal reason, such as a legal request, or at the Subscriber's request while it arranges to come back. We delete it once the reason ends.
  • What we keep after deletion. Our own business records about the Subscriber: a record that its account existed and was closed (its name, email address, Stripe account and customer IDs, the dates, and why); each payment it took through Poolnautics, without the payer's name or any End Customer details (the amount, date, Stripe IDs, and Poolnautics' fee); our bills to it, including Mail Service bills and letters, without recipients' names or addresses; and sales commission records. We keep these for as long as we need them for tax and accounting, card disputes, legal claims, and our legal obligations, which is generally at least seven years. We also keep the list of email addresses that bounced or reported our emails as spam, without the Subscriber's name, to protect email delivery for every Subscriber.
  • Backups and our providers. Deleted data remains in our database backups until they expire, within 7 days, and copies of photos held in Cloudflare's network expire within 7 days. Our email, error-monitoring, background-job, and printing-and-mailing providers keep their own logs (such as the address an email went to) for their own retention periods; we don't delete those when a Subscriber's account is deleted.

QuickBooks Online. Disconnecting QuickBooks, in Poolnautics or in QuickBooks, revokes Poolnautics' access at Intuit and deletes the access tokens at once. Poolnautics keeps the list of records it sent (their QuickBooks IDs) so that reconnecting the same QuickBooks company doesn't send them twice; that list is deleted when the Subscriber's account is deleted or when the Subscriber connects a different QuickBooks company. Deleting a Subscriber's account also revokes Poolnautics' access to its QuickBooks.
